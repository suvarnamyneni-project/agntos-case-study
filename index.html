UX case study · eXp Realty · Transactions Re-imagined · 2025–2026

One deal, every role, one operating system.

agntOS is the AI-native transaction and operations platform for agents and staff, in the US and Canada. I designed and prototyped it so a deal can move from submitted to paid in one shell, with a desk for every role along the way.

Problem

A deal moved from submitted to paid across about 11 tool types. Status was hard to see, so agents asked support where the deal was.

What I did

Mapped 14 journeys and 57 steps across 19 personas, wrote 40 flows plus two state models, and prototyped the desks in Claude Design, Lovable, Cursor, and Figma.

The design

One shell, many desks. The agent works from Home, Inbox, Transactions, and Doc AI. Staff work Task Center, Compliance, the TA desk, Canada, and Broker/Ops. AI reads and checks. A person decides.

Where it stands

Designed and prototyped. In the journey sheet, 38 of 57 steps were live in the settlement demo, 10 in progress, 5 proposed, and 4 still an open decision. Impact will be measured after launch.

Two deeper studies already exist: Deal Desk for settlement, and Agent Transactions for roles, permissions, and the back office. This page is the whole platform.

Context

A paid deal lived in several systems, and in the notes between them.

The Transactions Re-imagined program aimed at an AI-native transaction platform for the US and Canada, meant to eventually replace TRX and SkySlope. Agents were to work in Agent Desk inside the agent platform. Staff were to work in Deal Desk, with Task Center kept as its own app for the work queue.

Agents described an interface they disliked and required fields that did not belong on the deal. Some typed placeholder text to get past a screen. A rejected document did not ping them. MLS data was retyped. Logins were fragmented. The loudest complaint was the lack of one cross-transaction view of what they needed to do.

Staff pain sat in the gaps. Co-agent splits, advances, and transaction-coordinator fees arrived as free-text notes and were re-keyed. One settlement meant hopping between Task Center, Deal Desk, Doc AI, and SkySlope. Canada’s closing analysts shared spreadsheets with finance. After Settle, an accounting variance or an Oracle failure could stay invisible to the analyst. The payment question ran from the agent, to support, to the analyst, to production support, to accounting.

How might we carry one deal from submission to the accounting hand-off on one platform, for every role, with every number traceable and every error visible?

Who and how

Nineteen people, fourteen journeys, about eleven tools.

Five agent personas: listing agent, buyer’s agent, team lead, team member, referral agent. Fourteen staff personas: transaction coordinator, transaction analyst, compliance reviewer, closing analyst for Canada, settlement reviewer, manager, broker, operations, approver, accounting, post-close specialist, production support, customer support, and onboarding and training. I used the set as shared language with product and operations.

Fourteen journeys, fifty-seven steps. Each step is tagged by actor, system, automation, and build status. After I audited the prototype against that sheet, coverage was 7 covered, 6 partial, and 1 missing. The missing journey is J11, a combined statement and check file. The journeys include documents arriving and a task being created, “I haven’t been paid,” an extracted value that disagrees with the deal, oldest-first queue order, fewer screens and fewer trips into SkySlope, and Canada’s closing analyst settling the file.

Decisions that shaped the UI

  • It lives inside the agent platform shell.
  • Supabase and row-level security, so the UI matches the API for each persona.
  • Workflows, core, repeatable, and attempt, replace checklists. A sale attempt can be discarded while the listing persists.
  • Only approval steps are unskippable.
  • Doc AI is asynchronous, on the order of 30 seconds, so the screen needs a processing state.
  • Settled files are append-only. A correction is its own flow.
  • Task Center stays a separate app. Deep links carry the role and the transaction.

My role / scopeI defined the role-desk information architecture, the shared lifecycle, and the Task Center exception model, and mapped the architecture constraints onto screens.

Design system

Calm surfaces, loud status.

I own the agntOS design system. It is a monorepo: @agntos/tokens turns JSON into CSS variables, a TypeScript module, a Tailwind preset, and a contrast report. @agntos/ui is React and Storybook, with a light and dark toolbar and the accessibility addon.

Foundations are an ivory canvas, #f7f5f0, and a dark canvas, #0d0f14, with foreground #1b1f2a. Six hue pairs carry soft fills: accent, sky, sage, amber, clay, and violet. Violet is the AI hue. Type is Inter, Inter Tight for stats, Cormorant Garamond for display, and Geist Mono. Spacing is on a 4 pixel scale, controls use a 10 pixel radius, cards use 24, elevation is tinted, and focus is a 3 pixel ring.

About 40 components, each with states, keyboard behavior, ARIA, a story, and a unit test. Two of them matter more than the rest for this product: Disabled-with-reason, and a reject dialog that requires a reason. Patterns include AI document review (Passed, Suggestions, Couldn’t review), approval queue cards that surface the oldest age, urgency-ordered task rows, task detail, the approve and reject bar, a FINTRAC identity checklist, and the demo role switcher.

Principles: one chip per file, urgency first, explainable AI so Passed and Approved stay different words, and role-aware controls. When a control is disabled, the screen says why. Voice is sentence case. Name the file, the person, and the date. Status labels stay fixed. Primary text contrast in the report is 15.1:1 in light and 16.5:1 in dark. Small-text sage, amber, and clay badges miss AA, so those hues are kept off body copy.

My role / scopeI own the system. I extracted tokens from the deployed demo, defined the components and patterns, and documented the principles, voice, and accessibility.

AI patterns

AI reads and checks. A person decides.

Doc AI meets the agent at creation. Start from a document, then review the extraction. A missing signature is a hard block, with re-upload or an override recorded against a named person. Provenance is a chip on the value: Agent-provided, Extracted, TA-entered, or Deal notes. Financials open with the line “prefilled from the agent desk and extracts, edit only what changed.”

When two sources disagree, Review and choose shows both values with who entered each one, when, and the document page and section. The analyst picks. Which source wins by default is still an open decision. Critical alerts gate Send DA and say why. An informational alert, such as a mentor minimum, stays on the file and does not block. The alert can open a guided fix in place. A workflow guide on the right walks review alerts, confirm compensation, the DA, payments, settle, and post-settlement.

Gates fail closed. The payee total matches expected funds, close dates match, and variance is zero before the file can be marked disbursed. Compliance is a person in the loop: queue, review the checks, then Approve, Request changes, or Escalate, with a reason and an audit line. The long-term map puts an AI reviewer in front, commenting and flagging first, with auto-approval and auto-settle in a later phase when clear-to-settle documents match and no flags remain. People would then see escalations. Integration failures become owned tasks in Task Center, with a correlation ID. The screen shows a payload reference.

There is a second use of AI, and it stays separate from the product. Grok helped me read the archive. Claude, Lovable, Cursor, and GitHub helped me prototype. A fast prototype can still be wrong, so every build was audited against the journey sheet.

My role / scopeI designed the AI review, provenance, alert, and gate patterns and their states, and wrote the rule that AI checks and people decide.

One deal, end to end

Follow one file through the shell.

Six stops. Each one is a short stretch of the 79-screen walkthrough. Settlement’s rationale, the systems-truth rules, and the journey-by-journey audit are on Deal Desk, so they are linked here and not repeated.

  1. 01

    The agent starts the deal

    Home answers where the business stands, and it labels commission as an estimate until a file is paid. A new transaction can start from a document. Doc AI fills what it can read and blocks on a missing signature. The same home is where the file goes to the desk, with one Submit to Deal Desk. The agent’s deal page uses the timeline staff use.

  1. 02

    The desk picks it up

    The analyst queue carries the next action on every row. Task Center is the spine for what the happy path cannot hold: a missing document, a duplicate check, a calculator failure, an Oracle hand-off, an API replay. Each of those is an owned task with a correlation ID. Document review lists the blockers before anyone is asked to settle.

  1. 03

    Compliance reviews

    The compliance queue is documents waiting for a person. Needs review, Escalated, and Approved are the tabs, and settlement on this desk is view-only. When the checks pass, Approve sends the file to the analyst. Request changes sends it back. Exceptions from other systems arrive with their source named.

  1. 04

    The analyst prepares the money

    The TA desk queue can be read oldest first. Filters follow the lifecycle, and Critical and Asked agent sit on the row with the age and the next action. Inside the file, a mismatch shows the agent-provided value and the extracted value before anyone acts. Financials arrive prefilled, with a source chip on each line. Splits keep the role they had in SkySlope.

    The long middle of this path, from queue to disbursed, including the rules the gates enforce, is written up on Deal Desk.

    See all 8 screens in this stretch

  1. 05

    DA, funds, and disbursement

    The disbursement package lists payees, roles, amounts, and methods. Confirm stays shut until the payee total matches expected funds. Settlement shows the check, prefilled from the check copy, and variance reads Balanced only when actual equals expected. When funds balance and the dates match, the file can be marked disbursed. A toast confirms the hand-off to the accounting service, and the file turns read-only. It leaves the analyst’s queue.

    See all 6 screens in this stretch

  1. 06

    After settlement, Canada, and oversight

    A settled file is append-only, so a correction is a separate request: a reason, when to use it, the party at fault, and a description. Some reasons escalate to Post Close. In the US, a manager approves an override when a trigger says so. On Canadian deals, every override waits for approval. Canada’s closing analyst confirms funds, the settlement date, the payees, and who is settling, and the file then becomes read-only and posts to accounting. Broker and operations see the pipeline through to Closed, Disbursed.

One prototype, both roles

The combined build put agent and staff on one shared state, with a role switcher. A role-by-role check found gaps. An agent could land in staff UI on someone else’s deal. Compliance actions could be disabled with no reason. Transaction-analyst controls were loose enough that Send DA could proceed with an open underpayment. Broker and operations had no oversight screen. The timeline held.

What changed because of that check: the transaction coordinator came off the staff role switcher, because a TC is not a TA. Every disabled Send control gained an inline reason. The mentor fee percent is shown on the deal. A change log took the place of manual notes. Review and choose took the place of retyping. Send DA waits on every critical alert. Disbursed waits on variance of zero, or an approved write-off. US approvals fire on triggers. Canada approves everything.

Against the demo, the audit was 6 covered, 9 partial, and 4 missing. Against the journeys, it was 7 covered, 6 partial, and 1 missing. The 12-tab back-office proposal and the roles-by-permissions matrix are on Agent Transactions.

Clickable the same day.

Claude Design carried the visual system and the long canvases. Agent, analyst, and compliance were each explored as a file of about 105 pages, plus the TA Desk prototype, the settlement-flow files, and the 7-slide happy path. Reflection

What I’ll carry to the next platform.

01

Map the systems, not just the screens.

The failures I kept finding were hand-offs between Doc AI, SkySlope, Task Center, the accounting service, and Oracle. A calmer screen on top of that hop is still a hop.

02

Write the rule before the picture.

Estimates until paid, append-only after disbursed, and a TA who is not a TC. Those rules decided more of the UI than the visual pass did.

03

Design the exception first.

The happy path is the rare file. Missing signatures, underpayments, and a failed posting are ordinary work, so they got a queue, an owner, and a reason.

04

A fast prototype can be confidently wrong.

Claude, Lovable, and Cursor made a desk clickable the same day. The journey sheet was the check that kept a placeholder from becoming a business rule.

05

Leave the disagreement on the table.

Which source wins, whether the countries share a cap, per-document or stage approval. Named and owned, those questions move. Buried, they come back as rework.
