Executive summary

Problem: to close one deal, agents and staff moved between about 11 tools. Fee details sat in free-text notes and got re-keyed. Staff spent most of their effort checking numbers rather than deciding, and agents could not see where their deal stood.

Solution: role-based queues, one deal spine from submitted to disbursed, AI document review that flags differences for a person to resolve, validation gates that stay locked until the checks pass, and one design system for agents and staff.

Key screens: agent home, create-from-document review, analyst queue (oldest first), deal overview with blockers, review and choose, financials, settlement, and the compliance queue.

Outcomes so far

I have research and coverage evidence: 19 personas, 14 journeys, 57 settlement steps and 40 flows mapped, plus a prototype check of 7 journeys covered, 6 partial and 1 missing. The impact numbers are expected outcomes with targets, not results.

1. Project overview

AgentOS is the brokerage's "Transactions Re-imagined" program, built to replace the legacy transaction and document tools with one platform. I led the staff side: the work a Transaction Analyst does to move a deal from the queue to disbursed. That included the exceptions, the compliance hand-off, and the agent's view of the same timeline.

Business outcome I designed for: fewer screens per deal, a single source of truth for price, commission and dates, and a path toward auto-settling clean files so that analysts mostly handle exceptions.

What I owned, and what I did not

  • Owned: analyst queue, deal overview, alerts and blockers, review and choose, financials, disbursement, settlement, exceptions and recovery.
  • Owned: the agntOS design system (tokens, components, patterns, guidelines, Storybook).
  • Contributed: agent home, create-from-document, and the deal timeline on the same system.
  • Not mine: admin and backend configuration screens (led by another designer), and the accounting service itself (engineering).

I designed the deal spine with the exception lane drawn at the same time as the happy path. The sources used five different names for the end state, so I built one lifecycle and an alias table that product, engineering and operations could all point to.

Edge cases I designed for: a changed close date (the DA is revised automatically), a file with no DA needed (Skip), an underpayment (disbursed only at zero variance or with an approved write-off), a posting failure (retry versus restart), and a sale that falls through while the listing stays open.

Accessibility: I used WCAG 2.2 AA contrast checks, a 3px focus ring, keyboard behavior and ARIA on every component, and a reason on every disabled control. [TO CONFIRM: responsive or mobile behavior of the desk screens themselves]

5. AI product strategy and design

AI belongs where the input is unstructured and varied: documents. Rules belong where the answer must be exact: fees, balances and dates. A person decides wherever sources disagree or money moves.

What AI does, what rules do, and what a person decides
TaskApproachWhy
Classify documents, extract dates, amounts and check detailsAIMany document formats; this is the repetitive reading that slowed analysts down
Signatures and required documentsAI, with a hard blockFlagging is automatic. Re-uploading or overriding is a recorded human action.
Fees: $100 on a non-preferred advance, $50 on extend-a-hand, mentor fee waived under $250RulesDeterministic, so AI would only add risk
Funds balance, dates match, variance is zeroRules (gates)These have to fail closed
Which value wins on a mismatchHumanNo agreed rule exists, so a person chooses
Overrides and correctionsHuman, with a reasonNeeds an audit trail and approval on set triggers
Human and AI, stage by stage
StagePersonAI or systemReview pointIf AI is unsure or down
CreateAgent uploadsExtracts fields in about 30 seconds (async)"Review what we found" before submitStart from scratch
IntakeAnalystChecks signatures, checklist and duplicatesAnalyst marks the checklist complete"Couldn't review" state, then a manual check
ResolveAnalystFlags document vs deal mismatchesReview and choose, with source and pageAsk the agent
SettleAnalystReads the check (planned)Dates and balance gatesAnalyst types the check, marked as entered by a person
After settleProduction supportDetects posting failuresRetry, restart or escalateTime-boxed not-paid flag
  • Sources: each extracted value cites the document, page and section, and carries a provenance chip.
  • Confidence: the AI reports three outcomes (Passed, Suggestions, Couldn't review), kept separate from Approved and Rejected. [TO CONFIRM: show a numeric confidence score?]
  • Editable: the analyst picks a value instead of retyping it, and commission recalculates from that choice.
  • Confirmation: Send and Settle unlock only after the checks pass. Overrides are recorded.
  • Recovery: a disbursed file is append-only, and corrections are new records with a reason.
  • Fallback: if document AI is down, the orchestrator resends and the analyst can enter values by hand.
  • Privacy: payload references are shown, not the payloads. Demo data is synthetic. [TO CONFIRM: PII rules for AI processing]

7. Design system and developer handoff

I built the agntOS design system from tokens taken from the deployed demo: light and dark themes, six color pairs with soft fills, a 4px spacing unit, 10px control and 24px card radii, seven button variants, and motion durations. It ships as CSS variables, a Tailwind preset and a React library, with Storybook and unit tests.

The light-theme success, waiting and danger badge colors measure 4.15, 3.82 and 4.10 against their soft fills. They pass AA for large text but not for small text, so the system never uses them for body copy.

8. Outcomes and learnings

Expected outcomes. Nothing here has been measured in production.
Expected outcomeMetric to track after launch
Fewer screens per dealScreens per settlement versus the legacy flow
Faster settlementMedian time from submitted to settled
Fewer "where is my deal?" contactsSupport contacts per 100 deals
Trusted AI reviewCorrection rate on extracted fields, plus the share of files that still need a full human review
Exceptions handled earlierPosting failures caught by support before an agent reports them

Proof so far: 19 personas, 14 journeys, 57 settlement steps (38 live in the demo, 10 in progress, 5 proposed, 4 open), 40 flows, 24 open questions and 10 conflicts between sources, all logged.

  1. Design the exception first. Recovery flows are where people come to trust the system.
  2. Write the rule before drawing the screen: estimate until settled, append-only after.
  3. A fast AI prototype can be confidently wrong. The journey sheet kept the clicks honest.
  4. Leave disagreements visible. Open questions with owners moved faster than a screen that quietly picked a side.

Next I would build the real Settle step, the post-settlement error flag, manager approval with the Canada approve-all flow, and handling for a combined statement-and-check file.
