AgentOS

One deal. Every role. One connected operating system.

Reimagining real estate transaction management through an AI-native, role-based platform that connects agents, transaction analysts, compliance teams and operations across the entire deal lifecycle.

Design Problem

A deal crossed separate tools and roles, and nobody shared one answer for where the file was.

  • Retyping Agents re-entered listing data, and fees arrived as notes.
  • No next step A rejected document did not tell the agent what to do.
  • Hand checks Analysts compared dates and amounts by hand.
  • Hidden failure After settlement, an error could stay invisible to the person the agent calls.

Stakeholders

I was the sole designer on AgentOS, with the people who owned the program around the work.

  • Product Product managers, product owners, and stakeholders.
  • Build Engineering and QA.
  • The desk Analysts, compliance, and operations who run the file.
  • The file Agents, and the coordinators those agents hire.

End-users

The people on a file are not one role, and the coordinator is not the analyst.

  • Agents Listing and buyer’s agents, team leads, and referral agents.
  • Coordinator Hired by the agent and paid on the file. Stays with the agent.
  • Analyst Staff. Owns the queue, the exceptions, and the payee package.
  • Compliance Reviews documents. Settlement is not theirs to edit.

Primary Research

What I saw firsthand. I did not run interviews or usability tests.

  • Working sessions I sat in stakeholder working sessions, including “Documents arrive, I can see my tasks.”
  • Settlement demo I watched the settlement flow demonstrated end to end.
  • Open rules I took rules that were still open back to their owners.

Secondary Research

I studied the domain, the workflow, and the documents from the archive.

  • Archive Discovery notes and the program document.
  • Workflows I walked the paths in that archive, from submit through close.
  • Documents A missing signature or a mismatch is what stops a file.
  • US and Canada Canada uses a lawyer or conveyancer, and a closing analyst.

Competitive Analysis

I compared how three transaction platforms handle the same jobs: starting a deal, getting documents onto the checklist, and getting through compliance.

Platform What they do well Where it breaks down
Legacy document-centric tool Clear actions to start a document Agents split and assign pages to checklist items by hand; tasks sit in a passive tab and nothing checks the file before a human reviewer.
Real Brokerage (reZEN) AI checks a contract on upload and sorts documents onto the checklist Starting or converting a deal is still a long form wizard; the AI mostly acts as a help search.
Lone Wolf Pulls MLS and tax data; due dates tied to contract milestones Files organised like a filing cabinet; actions buried in dashboard widgets.

What AgentOS does differently

  • Start with a command, not a form. The agent types or says "Start a buyer transaction for Oak Avenue" instead of stepping through a wizard.
  • Ask only for what's missing. AI pre-fills from MLS, tax records and the uploaded agreement, then asks only for terms it can't find.
  • Documents file themselves. Drop in the signed agreement; AgentOS reads it, fills the deal and attaches it to the right checklist item.
  • Compliance before review. Missing signatures or fields are flagged right away, and the file can't go to the broker until fixed. The agent still approves every draft.

User Personas

The people I designed for. Role names are generalized.

Agent

Starts the file and wants to see what to do next, without retyping the listing.

Transaction analyst

Works the queue, clears exceptions, and confirms who gets paid.

Compliance

Reviews documents and approves or sends them back. Does not edit settlement.

Operations

Watches the pipeline and what happens after the analyst settles.

Design Decisions

The calls I would walk through.

  • Processing
  • Extracted
  • Mismatch
  • Couldn’t review
  • Override
  • Passed
  • Approval

One timeline

Problem. Status lived in phone calls.

Decision. Agent and staff share one timeline.

Why. Everyone looks at the same status.

The coordinator stays with the agent

Problem. The coordinator was easy to treat as staff.

Decision. They stay on the agent side. The analyst owns the staff steps and the payee package.

Why. A coordinator is hired by the agent and paid on the file.

AI assists. Humans stay in control.

Problem. A silent pass hides a field that was not checked.

Decision. AI reads and checks. A person reviews. States run from Processing and Extracted, at high or low confidence, through Mismatch, Couldn’t review, Override, and Passed.

Why. Passed is not Approved. Approval is a separate human step.

Show the source, and fail closed

Problem. Figures were retyped from notes, and money could move while something was wrong.

Decision. Each value names its source. A critical alert blocks until a person clears it.

Why. The reason stays with the choice.

Money is provisional until it is paid

Problem. An estimate was easy to read as final.

Decision. Figures stay estimates until Closed (Paid). Disbursed waits until the funds and the dates agree.

Why. Settlement and payment are not the same moment.

Outcome

Designed and prototyped, not launched.

  • Shared timeline The agent and staff follow the same file.
  • Human approval A person still approves, overrides, and settles.
  • Owned exceptions A failure keeps an owner instead of disappearing.
