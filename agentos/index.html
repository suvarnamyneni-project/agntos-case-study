Case study

AgentOS

One deal. Every role. One connected operating system.

Reimagining real estate transaction management through an AI-native, role-based platform that connects agents, transaction analysts, compliance teams and operations across the entire deal lifecycle.

I was the sole designer. Deal Desk is the staff and settlement desk inside AgentOS. Screens use fictional demo data. Full case study.

Design Problem

A deal crossed separate tools and roles, and nobody shared one answer to “where is my deal?”

  • Retyping Agents re-entered listing data, and fees arrived as notes.
  • No next step A rejected document did not tell the agent what to do.
  • Hand checks Analysts compared dates and amounts by hand.
  • Hidden failure After settlement, an error could stay invisible to the person the agent calls.

Stakeholders

I was the sole designer on AgentOS, with the people who owned the program around the work.

  • Product Product managers, product owners, and stakeholders.
  • Build Engineering and QA.
  • The desk Analysts, compliance, and operations who run the file.
  • The file Agents, and the coordinators those agents hire.

End-users

The people on a file are not one role, and the coordinator is not the analyst.

  • Agents Listing and buyer’s agents, team leads, and referral agents.
  • Coordinator Hired by the agent and paid on the file. Stays with the agent.
  • Analyst Staff. Owns the staff steps and the queue.
  • Compliance and operations Documents on one side, money on the other.

Primary Research

Secondhand input from the people in the work, plus time in the room. I did not run interviews or usability tests.

  • War rooms Stakeholder sessions, including “Documents arrive, I can see my tasks.”
  • Walkthroughs I walked the workflows from the archive, not from a live test.
  • Sources Discovery notes, the program document, and a settlement demo.
  • Check Every rule went back to its source before it reached a screen.

Secondary Research

I studied the domain, the workflow, and the documents a file depends on.

  • Workflow Submit, review, settle, disburse, and what happens after close.
  • Documents Where a missing signature or a mismatch stops the file.
  • Two countries Canada uses a lawyer or conveyancer and a closing analyst. A renamed US screen would have been wrong.
  • Open questions Which source wins, and whether approval is per document or per stage, stayed open.

Competitive Analysis

A small, qualitative look at the kind of tool this is for. Not a vendor scorecard, and not a measured comparison.

Existing transaction tools

  • Each step tends to live in its own place.
  • Status and money get retyped between people.
  • A document check and the decision can look like the same moment.

AgentOS

  • One platform, a desk for each role, one timeline.
  • The source of a figure stays on the line.
  • Reading a document is not the same as approving it.

User Personas

Four people I designed for. Role names are generalized.

Agent

Starts the file and wants one view of what to do next, without retyping the listing.

Transaction analyst

Works the queue, clears exceptions, and needs the source of each figure.

Compliance

Reviews documents and approves or sends them back. Settlement is not theirs to edit.

Operations / settlement

Confirms who gets paid, and that the funds agree, before the file can be marked disbursed.

Design Decisions

The calls I would walk through in an interview.

One timeline

Problem. Status lived in phone calls. Decision. Agent and staff share one timeline. Why. That is the answer to “where is my deal?”

The coordinator stays with the agent

Problem. The coordinator was easy to treat as staff. Decision. They stay on the agent side. The analyst owns the staff steps. Why. A coordinator is hired by the agent and paid on the file.

AI assists. Humans stay in control.

Problem. A silent pass hides a field that was not checked. Decision. AI reads and checks. A person reviews. States are Processing, Extracted at high or low confidence, Mismatch, Couldn’t review, and Override. Approval is human. Why. Passed is not Approved.

Show the source, and fail closed

Problem. Figures were retyped from notes, and money could move while something was wrong. Decision. Each value names its source, and a critical alert blocks until a person clears it. Why. The reason stays with the choice.

Money is provisional until it is paid

Problem. An estimate was easy to read as final. Decision. Figures stay estimates until Closed (Paid). Disbursed waits until the funds and the dates agree. Why. Settlement and payment are not the same moment.

Outcome

Designed and prototyped, not launched.

  • Enables One timeline from the agent’s home through settlement.
  • Enables A person still approves, overrides, and settles.
  • Enables An exception has an owner instead of disappearing.
  • Next Sit with analysts before another visual pass. Which source wins is still open.
